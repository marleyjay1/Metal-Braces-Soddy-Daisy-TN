What Are Metal Braces?

Metal braces are a traditional orthodontic treatment that uses small metal brackets, an archwire, and other components to gradually move teeth into improved positions. The orthodontist adjusts the system throughout treatment to guide tooth movement and improve the way the upper and lower teeth fit together.

Modern metal braces are designed with patient comfort and effective tooth movement in mind. They remain a popular option for families because they can address a wide range of orthodontic concerns.

Who May Need Metal Braces?

You may benefit from an orthodontic evaluation if you notice any of the following concerns:

  • Crowded or overlapping teeth
  • Gaps between teeth
  • Crooked or rotated teeth
  • An overbite, underbite, or crossbite

  • Teeth that are difficult to clean because of their positioning

Parents may also consider an orthodontic evaluation for their children as their permanent teeth develop. An early assessment does not always mean braces are needed immediately, but it can help identify concerns that may require monitoring or treatment.

What to Expect During Metal Braces Treatment

Understanding the treatment process can help you and your family feel more prepared before beginning orthodontic care.

Initial Orthodontic Consultation

Your orthodontist will examine your teeth, bite, and overall oral health. Photographs or dental X-rays may be recommended when appropriate. The examination helps determine whether metal braces are suitable and what improvements may be achievable.

Braces Placement

If you decide to proceed, the orthodontic team will attach brackets to your teeth and connect them with an archwire. The appointment typically takes less than a couple of hours, although the exact time can vary.

Regular Adjustment Visits

Throughout treatment, you will attend periodic appointments so your orthodontist can assess progress and make necessary adjustments. Keeping these appointments helps your treatment stay on track.

Retainers After Treatment

Once your braces are removed, retainers help maintain your results. Following your orthodontist's instructions for retainer wear is important because teeth can shift after active treatment.

Caring for Your Metal Braces

Good oral hygiene is essential throughout orthodontic treatment. Food particles can become trapped around brackets and wires, increasing the risk of plaque buildup, cavities, and gum inflammation.

  • Brush carefully after meals whenever possible.
  • Use floss threaders or orthodontic flossing tools to clean between teeth.
  • Avoid hard, sticky, and chewy foods that may damage brackets or wires.
  • Attend regular dental checkups and professional cleanings.
  • Contact your orthodontic office if a bracket becomes loose or a wire causes persistent irritation.

Mild soreness can occur after braces are first placed or adjusted. Soft foods and following your orthodontist's instructions may help you manage temporary discomfort.
